# Find and reuse efficient methods

[한국어](efficient-work.md)

Efficiency means the total burden of reaching the user's required outcome and quality. Do not optimize technology count, fewer calls, tokens, or brevity in isolation. Finishing useful work comes first.

Respect existing goals, deadlines, budgets, and preferences without repeated questions. When unclear but work can proceed, choose a simple quality-preserving method and state material assumptions. Small tasks need no measurement table or permanent settings.

Reject faster methods that fail required correctness/completeness, uncertainty/conflict disclosure, provenance/version/scope, independent interpretation, access/execution/transmission rules, privacy, or recovery/data preservation. Compare time, cost, rework, and maintenance among methods meeting those conditions. Do not invent a weighted score across unlike units; disclose tradeoffs.

## A small bounded comparison

1. Check authorized project experience for matching workload, purpose, source/definition versions, model/tools, permissions, scale, and quality. Do not gather other workspaces' notes or apply mismatched experience blindly.
2. Identify observed time, calls, failed retrieval, rework, or cost. Do not claim tokens, money, or accuracy that tools did not measure. Finish one-off work directly when existing search is sufficient.
3. Start with current behavior and one promising alternative: better queries/filters/references, fewer tools, an existing-index rebuild. Add DB/RAG/embeddings only for a demonstrated need.
4. Set scope, representative sample, and stop conditions before testing. Follow stated time/cost/call/trial budgets. Experiment copies, data replication, package installs, and model calls need the same permissions as ordinary work. Start small when no budget is specified; do not silently expand into full-data or paid experiments.
5. Compare the same sources, definitions, tasks, permissions, and important failure cases. Do not invent accuracy where correct answers/evidence cannot be checked. Treat small unrepresentative samples as exploratory.
6. Separate setup/install/migration/indexing, query/generation, review/rework, and updating/maintenance. Distinguish observed and expected future usage. Note cache/order/device/network/tool/model differences. One fast query does not establish lower total cost.
7. Select a meaningful quality-preserving improvement, or keep the simpler current method when differences are small/uncertain. Real configuration changes follow [adaptation authority](adaptive-environment.en.md). Simple method selection needs no unnecessary environment change or reapproval.

Do not cherry-pick runs or generalize one fast result. Stop experiments when measurement cost outweighs expected benefit, quality fails, assumptions change, or budget is exhausted.

## Conditional experience, not model training

Only retain reusable observations when useful and authorized, for example under `.evidence-first/learning/`, using the [optional template](../assets/efficiency-note-template.en.md). Record context, baseline/candidate, measurement method/results, setup/maintenance burden, decision, uncertainty, failures, and revisit conditions. Avoid sensitive source text, credentials, unnecessary question transcripts, and private metadata in public material.

Reuse a method when conditions still match instead of rerunning the same benchmark every task. Reassess after workload, source/definition, scale, quality, model/tool, permission, or observed-failure changes. Keep rejected/failed alternatives with their conditions to avoid repeated unsuccessful adoption.

These are scoped external work notes, not model-weight updates, universal ontology facts, or new permission. User choices and higher-priority instructions remain authoritative.
